# Art direction and handheld references

These are curated source pointers and our studio's suggested applications,
not substitutes for the original works. Select one or two entries for the
current uncertainty; do not fetch the entire library. The local application
notes work offline. Open the source before attributing detailed claims to it.
For a video, inspect relevant footage/transcript and record timestamps; for a
visual reference, inspect the image at gameplay scale. If access is unavailable,
state that limitation and use the notes as hypotheses, not reviewed evidence.
Paid books are optional: use an available authorized copy or the free alternatives.
Record only the source used, the decision it informed, and the test/result in
the task's existing design notes. Do not claim playtesting from reading alone.

## Build a readable visual language

[Dota 2 Workshop visual references](https://www.dota2.com/workshop/build/references?l=english)
— Valve. Official web guides; follow Character Art Guide. The old PDF is a redirect.

- Consult for silhouette, value hierarchy, and recognizable forms at game distance.
- Application: extract readability principles, not Dota's aesthetic or production
  requirements. Give player, hazard, and goal distinguishable shapes and emphasis.
- Experiment: compare the proof assets as silhouettes and in grayscale at phone
  scale; check whether their roles remain distinguishable.

[Lospec Palette List](https://lospec.com/palette-list) — Lospec/community contributors.
Free palette library.

- Consult when defining a constrained palette rather than collecting mood words.
- Application: assign background, player, hazard, goal, and accent roles; record
  the chosen palette and any modifications. Do not rely on hue alone for state.
- Experiment: render a gameplay frame with two candidate palettes and compare
  contrast and focal hierarchy in color and grayscale.

[Game UI Database](https://www.gameuidatabase.com/) — Edd Coates.
Visual reference website; automated fetching may be restricted.

- Consult for HUD, settings, results, and navigation examples once needed.
- Application: inspect a few comparable screens, annotate what serves their
  context, and adapt the pattern to our mechanic. Popularity is not quality evidence.
- Experiment: place the proposed UI over gameplay at actual size; check hierarchy,
  occlusion, and touch reach. If images cannot be viewed, do not claim a visual audit.

## Design for the handheld device

[Design great interfaces for handheld games](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/meet-with-apple/243/)
— Apple. Free video with web transcript.

- Consult when defining phone framing, flexible layouts, or touch interaction.
- Application: connect layout and legibility decisions to screen size and input;
  use the transcript for guidance and footage for visual examples.
- Experiment: try the proof frame at the target aspect ratios and on a phone;
  check safe areas, thumb coverage, and readable interactive elements.

[Game controls](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/game-controls)
— Apple. Living platform documentation.

- Consult before committing to touch-control placement or interaction conventions.
- Application: favor mappings appropriate to the mechanic; compare direct
  manipulation with virtual controls. Recheck current guidance for platform specifics.
- Experiment: perform the core action one-handed on hardware and record misses,
  occlusion, and uncomfortable reaches; route behavioral tuning to game-feel.

[Game Accessibility Guidelines](https://gameaccessibilityguidelines.com/)
— collaborative studio, specialist, and academic guidance. Free web checklist.

- Consult when checking whether visual and feedback decisions exclude players.
- Application: start with relevant basic guidance for controls, text, contrast,
  motion, and alternate information channels; adapt it to the game's demands.
- Experiment: test the same scene with sound off and reduced motion; verify
  essential states have another readable cue and report any untested checks.
