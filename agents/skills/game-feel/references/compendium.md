# Feel references

These are curated source pointers and our studio's suggested applications,
not substitutes for the original works. Select one or two entries for the
current uncertainty; do not fetch the entire library. The local application
notes work offline. Open the source before attributing detailed claims to it.
For a video, inspect relevant footage/transcript and record timestamps; for a
visual reference, inspect the image at gameplay scale. If access is unavailable,
state that limitation and use the notes as hypotheses, not reviewed evidence.
Paid books are optional: use an available authorized copy or the free alternatives.
Record only the source used, the decision it informed, and the test/result in
the task's existing design notes. Do not claim playtesting from reading alone.

## Layer feedback deliberately

[Juice It or Lose It](https://gdcvault.com/play/1016789/Juice-It-or-Lose)
— Martin Jonasson and Petri Purho. Free GDC talk; player access may vary.

- Consult when a readable, responsive core action still feels flat.
- Application: map feedback to the event it communicates; add layers individually
  so their contribution can be judged. More effects are not automatically better.
- Experiment: compare the baseline with one added sound, deformation, or particle
  response; retain it only if impact improves without obscuring play.

[The Art of Screenshake](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AJdEqssNZ-U)
— Jan Willem Nijman. Video demonstration.

- Consult for incremental changes to perceived force and responsiveness.
- Application: examine the combined role of motion, impact, sound, and camera;
  avoid interpreting the title as a universal prescription for heavy shake.
- Experiment: compare one change with effects off and at a reduced intensity;
  check both perceived impact and the ability to track the next target.

## Treat the camera as a separate system

[Juicing Your Cameras](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2016/Presentations/Eiserloh_Squirrel_JuicingYourCameras.pdf)
— Squirrel Eiserloh. Free GDC slide PDF.

- Consult when tracking, shake, or settling makes the game uncomfortable or unclear.
- Application: separate camera tracking from transient feedback; expose intensity
  and decay, cap their combined motion, and preserve a stable reduced-motion mode.
- Experiment: replay the same event sequence with tracking only, feedback only,
  and both; check target visibility and settling before the next decision.

## Audit action acknowledgment

[The Lens of Juiciness](https://schellgames.com/blog/the-lens-of-juiciness)
— Jesse Schell / Schell Games. Free short article.

- Consult when input acknowledgment or secondary motion is inconsistent.
- Application: audit continuous actions as well as isolated hits; feedback should
  explain the player's control and the system's response.
- Experiment: trace press/hold/release and success/failure events; remove sound
  and haptics and verify the interaction is still understandable.
