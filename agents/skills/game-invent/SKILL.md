# Invent game

Honor the brief's platform, audience, controls, and scope. State assumptions
where unspecified; default to one-hand mobile play and a two-day prototype.
Start from player decisions, not themes, progression, or monetization.

## Explore combinations

Combine **interaction + physical/system concept + unusual constraint**.
Use these as seeds, not a closed menu:

| Interaction | Physical/system concept | Unusual constraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Drag, trace, hold/release, flick, rotate | Elasticity, resonance, pressure, momentum | You control an anchor, not the moving object |
| Place, connect, split, redirect | Flow, feedback, conservation, chain reactions | Every action consumes or transfers the same resource |
| Scrub, alternate, rhythmically pulse | Delay, phase, propagation, equilibrium | Your last action repeats after a delay |

Explore several combinations and keep **3–5 mechanically distinct concepts**.
The constraint must change decisions, not merely the fiction. Remove themes
and compare the rules; discard reskins and combinations that add explanation
without producing an interesting tradeoff.

## Deliver each concept

- **Name and combination:** one sentence describing the playable premise.
- **Player action → immediate result:** exact gesture, controlled variable, and
  visible consequence, including the first five seconds of play.
- **Repeatable loop:** objective, failure/recovery, and why performing the action
  repeatedly is satisfying without rewards or unlocks.
- **Mastery:** what an expert perceives or controls that a beginner does not.
- **Difficulty scaling:** one or two rule parameters; explain how they demand
  better decisions instead of only faster reactions or more content.
- **Mechanical distinction:** closest known analogue and the changed decision;
  explain why this is not reducible to generic tap/jump timing. A tap is fine
  when it manipulates a distinctive system. Label unverified novelty claims.
- **Smallest proof:** the scene, rules, and placeholder assets needed in at most
  two days, plus one playtest question that could disprove the fun hypothesis.

End with a recommended first prototype, its biggest uncertainty, and why it
has the best ratio of interesting decisions to implementation effort. Suggest
`game-evaluate` for deeper comparison; do not expand into a production plan.
