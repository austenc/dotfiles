# Evaluation references

These are curated source pointers and our studio's suggested applications,
not substitutes for the original works. Select one or two entries for the
current uncertainty; do not fetch the entire library. The local application
notes work offline. Open the source before attributing detailed claims to it.
For a video, inspect relevant footage/transcript and record timestamps; for a
visual reference, inspect the image at gameplay scale. If access is unavailable,
state that limitation and use the notes as hypotheses, not reviewed evidence.
Paid books are optional: use an available authorized copy or the free alternatives.
Record only the source used, the decision it informed, and the test/result in
the task's existing design notes. Do not claim playtesting from reading alone.

## Focus the critique

[The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses](https://schellgames.com/art-of-game-design/)
— Jesse Schell. Paid book with companion resources.

- Consult when the scorecard identifies a weakness but not a useful question.
- Application: choose a few relevant perspectives, such as challenge, player
  choice, or the core toy. Do not apply the full deck or invent quotations.
- Experiment: use a chosen lens to propose one rule change, then compare player
  behavior before and after. Free fallback: the skill's existing scorecard.

## Iterate around players

[Game Design Workshop](https://www.tracyfullerton.com/writing)
— Tracy Fullerton. Paid book; author page links to the work.

- Consult for a deeper player-centered prototyping and evaluation process.
- Application: make the experience goal explicit, prototype cheaply, observe
  actual play, and revise based on evidence rather than the designer's explanation.
- Experiment: test the stripped-down loop with an unfamiliar player and record
  where their interpretation differs from the intended action/consequence.
  Use the free playtest-method article below if the book is unavailable.

## Match method to uncertainty

[Choose the right playtest method](https://gamesuserresearch.com/choose-the-right-playtest-method/)
— Steve Bromley / Games User Research. Free article; optional guide may require signup.

- Consult before treating a survey, observation, or interview as interchangeable.
- Application: name the decision first, then choose evidence suited to it.
  Observe comprehension and control errors; ask neutral follow-up questions
  about experience. Separate what players do from what they say they would do.
- Experiment: write one question and an observable criterion before the session;
  record behaviors and quotations separately from interpretation. A five-second
  comprehension check is our screening heuristic, not proof of lasting appeal.
