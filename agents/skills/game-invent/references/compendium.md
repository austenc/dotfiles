# Mechanic invention references

These are curated source pointers and our studio's suggested applications,
not substitutes for the original works. Select one or two entries for the
current uncertainty; do not fetch the entire library. The local application
notes work offline. Open the source before attributing detailed claims to it.
For a video, inspect relevant footage/transcript and record timestamps; for a
visual reference, inspect the image at gameplay scale. If access is unavailable,
state that limitation and use the notes as hypotheses, not reviewed evidence.
Paid books are optional: use an available authorized copy or the free alternatives.
Record only the source used, the decision it informed, and the test/result in
the task's existing design notes. Do not claim playtesting from reading alone.

## Rules and experience

[MDA](https://www.cs.northwestern.edu/~hunicke/MDA.pdf) — Robin Hunicke,
Marc LeBlanc, Robert Zubek. Free five-page PDF.

- Consult when a concept names a feeling without explaining how play creates it.
- Application: separate mechanics (rules), dynamics (behavior during play), and
  aesthetics (player experience, not just visual style). Write the causal link.
- Experiment: change one rule and predict which player behavior should change;
  compare the prediction with a prototype session.

## Expressive input constraints

[How to realize various actions in a one-button game](https://dev.to/abagames/how-to-realize-various-actions-in-a-one-button-game-fak)
— Kenta Cho. Free article.

- Consult when simple controls are producing generic timing-game concepts.
- Application: explore what pressing, holding, and releasing mean in different
  system states; keep the consequence legible rather than hiding extra commands.
- Experiment: build two mappings for the same system and test whether a new
  player can predict the consequence of each action.

[Crisp Game Lib](https://github.com/abagames/crisp-game-lib) — Kenta Cho.
Free browser games, source examples, and library documentation.

- Consult for concrete examples of small mechanics with minimal production cost.
- Application: play an example, inspect its rules in source, and extract its
  controlled variable, constraint, failure condition, and skill requirement.
  Invent a different decision structure; this is not an engine mandate.
- Experiment: replace one rule in an isolated sketch and compare the decisions
  it produces. Reading code alone does not establish whether it feels good.

## Emergence and scope

[The Lens of Emergence](https://schellgames.com/blog/lens-of-emergence)
— Jesse Schell / Schell Games. Free short article.

- Consult when depth seems to require more content or unlocks.
- Application: map verbs to objects and identify interacting consequences.
- Experiment: hold the verb count constant and add one interaction between
  existing rules; look for a new strategy rather than just more complexity.

[How to Prototype a Game in Under 7 Days](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/how-to-prototype-a-game-in-under-7-days)
— Kyle Gabler, Kyle Gray, Matt Kucic, Shalin Shodhan / Experimental Gameplay
Project. Free article.

- Consult when the proposed prototype resembles a small production release.
- Application: isolate one fun hypothesis, use placeholders, and cut everything
  that cannot help test it. Our two-day target is a studio adaptation.
- Experiment: list the minimum playable scene and a stopping criterion before
  building; end by deciding whether the hypothesis deserves another iteration.
